Plate 01

Three planes read one host

the same counters, three readers, three schedules

Where the planes come from

  • Plane 1 is vCenter's own history, shaped by its statistics level. Raising the level changes vCenter's charts and its vpxd load, and nothing downstream doc + live.
  • Plane 2 is the platform's input. The adapter reads through vStats at 20 seconds no matter what the level says, and both vCenters on the reference estate sit at Level 1 while Operations holds every counter it needs live.
  • Plane 3 is new in 9.1 and optional. It is a pull of its own with its own store, addressed per VCF instance, and it is the only plane that serves 2-second values doc + live.

What the counts look like on one small source

  • Real-Time Metrics catalog of 437 names; 175 collected for the management vCenter, 171 for the workload vCenter live.
  • Five acquisition profiles, all Essentials: ESXi 20 s (83 names), vCenter 20 s (46), vCenter 60 s (3), a PerformanceManager set served at 20 s although its profile name carries 300 (41), ESX Top 2 s (21, nineteen of them also under the 20 s profile) live.
  • Series on the management source: 5,386, of which 1,339 are the 2-second set; the workload source carries 2,927 live. A series count is an inventory measurement rather than a platform constant: the same read a week earlier returned 5,391 and 1,341 here, and 2,316 on the workload source before it grew. Read your own before you size anything on it.

Tradeoff

For the vSphere estate the collection problem is already solved by plane 2. A second 20-second pull from vCenter, whether by a warehouse or by a monitoring platform's vCenter integration, adds load on vpxd and truth to nothing, because the analytics store already keeps the 5-minute mean and the peak keys. The strategy question is therefore extraction, not collection: which plane, at which grain, for which decision.

Plate 02

What each store keeps and loses

the 5-minute value is a mean; the peak survives only in the peak keys or on the real-time plane

What survives, and where

  • The stored 5-minute value is the mean of the fifteen 20-second samples in the cycle ending at its timestamp, within 2 to 4 percent live.
  • Twelve VM keys keep the within-cycle maximum, the 20_sec_peak kind (CPU ready, co-stop, overlap, other wait; memory contention; network usage and packets per second; virtual-disk latency; the guest's run queue, disk queue, context switches, page-out rate); a host has none, and neither does a cluster. Twelve is the full set and not a guarantee: swept across sixty machines, 45 carry all twelve, 10 carry eight (the four guest keys absent, which is what a machine without guest-level collection looks like), and 5 carry one. Build a panel on the twelve and it renders empty on the machines that have fewer, so count them per machine before you promise the view. The keys named "highest of all instances" are the busiest disk, vCPU, core, or adapter averaged over the cycle, not a time peak. The one testable within-cycle key tracks the window maximum, and plate 11 shows the 20-second plane reproducing it doc + live.
  • Server-side hourly AVG and MAX are exact against the native points, so downsampling on the server costs no truth live.

What this settles for the raw-versus-real-time argument

  • Utilization, rightsizing, and capacity read means and long windows; the 5-minute grain serves them without loss that matters.
  • Contention bursts are the one thing the mean hides badly; they belong to the peak keys or to the real-time plane, for a scoped set, in region.
  • Live troubleshooting is not a BI decision; it rides the real-time plane and stays there.

Tradeoff

Pulling native 5-minute points for everything costs about twelve times the hourly tier for no decision gain, and it still does not recover peaks, because the peak was averaged away before it was stored. Spend native resolution only on the contention keys of a scoped hot-set, and take the peak keys where they exist.

Plate 03

Horizons and extraction deadlines

how long each grain exists anywhere, on a log scale

The settings behind the bars, as this instance reports them

  • TIME_SERIES_DATA_RETENTION_IN_MONTHS = 6, then ROLLUP_TIME_SERIES_DATA_RETENTION_IN_MONTHS = 36 live.
  • REALTIME_DATA_RETENTION_IN_DAYS = 15; ESX_TOP_DATA_RETENTION_IN_HOURS = 24 live. The 6-hour figure in the 9.1 pages is the ESX Top view's maximum time range, not retention doc.
  • The vCenter rollup ladder is documented; the real-time window is short and is not a store doc.
  • The platform will not tell you whether these are defaults. The settings API answers a key and its value and nothing else: there is no default beside it and no defaults endpoint to compare against, so an instance cannot be asked whether a retention setting was ever changed. That matters most when you inherit one. Record what you set, because the platform keeps no memory of what it started as live.

What lazy enforcement means for a pipeline

  • A served horizon can run past its setting for days and then drop in one step. Never plan on data older than the setting being there, and never assume a fixed cut.
  • Watermark on bucket-end timestamps and reconcile absence against inventory and collector health before calling a gap a decision.

Tradeoff

Longer retention on the platform buys extraction slack, not analysis: the warehouse still needs its own tiers. The cheaper move is to extract each grain once, well inside its horizon, and to keep the platform's retention at defaults unless a documented limit or a proven gap says otherwise.

Plate 04

Decisions and the grain they consume

granularity is a per-decision parameter, not a property of the pipeline

The platform already computes the decisions

  • Rightsizing is the maximum projected utilization out to 30 days past the policy's Time Remaining warning threshold, shaped by the policy risk level doc + live.
  • Sharing and reallocation have four public surfaces: reclaim per reason, rightsize per reason, workload placement, and the recommendation catalog (1,254 definitions here), plus 18 per-VM decision statkeys live.
  • Capacity ratios live in the policy (CPU 4:1, memory 1:1, disk 2:1 here); the risk level is a quantile dial on recency-weighted demand, so a warehouse percentile over a fixed window is a different estimator and will disagree by design.

Path chooser

  • Hypervisor-visible signal? Operations, extracted. Never a second pull.
  • A burst or contention question? Peak keys first; a scoped 20-second hot-set from Real-Time Metrics if the key does not exist.
  • Inside the guest? An in-guest agent. Off vSphere? Zabbix. Identity and business context? The CMDB.

Tradeoff

Recomputing a decision in the warehouse from raw counters buys independence at the price of disagreement with the platform's own number. Read the platform's decision, record the policy's risk level beside it as provenance, and compute only what the platform does not: cross-estate comparisons, cost, and history beyond its retention.

Plate 05

The catalog: what a warehouse asks for, and where it lives

nine families, the vCenter counter with its statistics level, the Operations key, the tier; every key checked against a live statkey list

What the levels settle

  • vCenter keeps a counter beyond its real-time window only at or above the counter's level, and the default is 1. Demand, co-stop, active and swapped memory, and the packet and drop counters sit at level 2; swap wait at 3; readiness and OS uptime at 4 live.
  • Operations reads every counter here through vStats at 20 seconds whatever the level says, so a list that would force the level up on vCenter is already served at level 1 by plane 2 live.
  • The peak keys are two kinds. Twelve 20_sec_peak keys on a VM keep the highest 20-second sample of the cycle; the peak_ keys, "Highest ... of all instances" in the definitions in VMware guidance, are the busiest disk, vCPU, core, or adapter averaged over the cycle, and a host has only those doc + live.

What only Operations or Tools produces

  • The guest family through VMware Tools: free, needed, and usable memory, swap space remaining, page rates, run and disk queues, context switches, filesystem totals. Conditional on Tools; a blank is absence, never zero live.
  • The decision keys: oversized and undersized with the reclaimable amounts, the recommended size under the policy's risk level, idle, snapshot space and count. Read them; never recompute them live.
  • Instanced families (virtual-disk latency, IOPS, outstanding IO; network packets and drops) carry the VM value under the Aggregate of all instances instance; the plain key returns nothing live.

Trap

A catalog lists more keys than a resource collects. On the reference VM, 273 statkeys are collected out of 983 in the adapter's VirtualMachine catalog, and seven of the nineteen catalog peak keys are not among them. Read a resource's own statkey list once and bind those names; a view, a super metric, or an extract bound to a catalog name renders a dash and teaches nothing.

The outage lesson

On the reference estate no metrics were stored for any vCenter adapter for nineteen days during a 9.1.0 upgrade with snapshot reverts. An extract without a reconciliation step would have landed zero demand for every object. A fleet-wide plateau in the age of the newest point is the signature of a collection outage, not of a quiet estate.

Plate 07

Decide in region, ship the thin layer

one Operations instance per region, the real-time plane per VCF instance, one collector per domain beyond vSphere, the warehouse in the middle

Limits in VMware guidance that bound the shape doc

  • One Operations instance per fleet; one fleet per region is a named scenario; the cloud proxy is mandatory in the first 9.1 instance and outbound only.
  • Unified proxy sizes: Small up to 16,000 VMs, Standard 16,000 to 80,000. Node limits from the 9.0 table: Medium 30,000 objects and 5 million metrics; Large 44,000 and 8 million; Extra Large 100,000 and 20 million.
  • Proxy to node: RTT under 50 ms, 15 to 200 Mbps. Persistence buffers at most one hour; a collector group fails over after 10 minutes.

Why shape B

  • A single global instance puts every region's collection behind one WAN and one node ceiling; a per-region instance keeps collection local and ships what is small.
  • The WAN carries decisions and hourly aggregates, which compress a 10,000-VM region to about 144 MB a day; native points would be twelve times that, and the real-time plane hundreds of times.
  • The same shape holds beyond vSphere: one collector per domain per region keeps the raw series local, computes the hourly statistics at the edge, and ships them as their own lane; not every domain has to pass through Operations to reach the warehouse (plate 08).

Tradeoff

Regional instances cost a second set of policies, groups, and dashboards to keep consistent, which is a content-management problem with a known cure (versioned, generated content). A global instance costs WAN bandwidth, a hard node ceiling, and an hour of buffer as the only protection against a link outage. The second cost is structural; the first is procedural.

Choosing the collector

  • One collector per domain, one agent per host. Two agents for one signal is the failure mode; an agent posting to Operations and a second agent do not share a guest.
  • Choose by where the data must live. When the data must exist in Operations, pick the door by source type: open-source Telegraf for guest counters, viewed there and shared with the application team; the builder for any API or Prometheus source; a stable pack where one exists; the API push for derived numbers. A neutral collector's own store when the data must not depend on the VCF stack, for residency, another team's ownership, or an estate that is mostly not vSphere.
  • Network hardware runs no agent. Poll it, subscribe to it, or receive its flows, from the domain collector or from a network-monitoring platform such as Broadcom's DX NetOps or AppNeta. VCF Operations for Networks reads the flows the VMware estate itself exports, vSphere Distributed Switch and NSX IPFIX, and is not the physical network's monitor. Inside Operations, the Network Devices pack follows DX NetOps as the operations lens on devices and the SNMP ingestion path.

What every domain ships

  • Hourly min, avg, max, count per series, computed where the collector already knows how: BasicStats, recording rules, or trends. The raw series stay in the domain's regional store, as native points stay in Operations.
  • A daily gold layer where the domain computes decisions of its own; otherwise the hourly tier is the whole feed.
  • Its own key: the vCenter pair for vSphere, a CI for a server or a device; cross-domain joins follow the CMDB's relationships.

Tradeoff

A neutral collector buys independence from the platform and one pipeline for every operating system and device, at the price of a second store to run and a second identity to keep straight. A door into Operations buys the one extraction contract for that domain, native rollups and the same key discipline, at the price of the node's object and metric limits and a dependency on the VCF stack. Both are sound; the builder's Prometheus source lets one collector serve both, and two agents on one host is what is not.

Plate 09

One key across every system

a managed object ID is unique only inside its vCenter; the pair is unique everywhere

Verified equalities live

  • Operations VMEntityObjectID equals the vCenter MOID; VMEntityVCID equals vCenter about.instanceUuid; VMEntityInstanceUUID equals the VM's config.instanceUuid.
  • Real-Time Metrics labels carry the MOIDs and the vCenter's name or IP, never its UUID; the UUID is the sourceId you pass, so stamp it.

CMDB side doc

  • The vCenter model keys a VM as a cmdb_ci_vmware_instance plus a guest server CI; the identifier fields are the vCenter UUID and the MOID.
  • The Operations management pack maps object_id from VMEntityObjectID and vcenter_uuid from VMEntityVCID, the same pair.

Trap

Keying on the MOID alone works until the second vCenter, and keying on the name works until the first rename. Both failures are silent: rows join to the wrong object and every decision downstream inherits it.

Plate 10

Reading Real-Time Metrics without getting burned

seven behaviors of the PromQL API that an extractor must encode, all measured

The extractor's seven lines. Pin the profile with a label matcher. Read cardinality with count by (profile) and partition by host so no call nears 101. Measure cadence as value-change spacing at a fine step. Stamp rows with sourceId and profile. Treat the truncation warning and an empty answer as errors to investigate, never as zeros. Write labels lowercase and take the names from the metadata endpoint, never from a picker. Never rate a gauge.

Plate 11

Two planes, one set of samples: the proof, and the calls

the store's mean and peak keys are rollups of the 20-second series; the seven calls a pipeline makes; ten queries built for the strategy, verified

What the comparison settles

  • The guest family matches exactly: mean and peak ratios of 1.00 on every one of 36 buckets for three VMs. The two planes are reading the same 20-second samples live.
  • Network usage sits at 0.96 to 0.98 for the mean and 0.95 to 0.97 for the peak: two collection paths sampling at different phases, not two truths live.
  • Two guest keys are normalized in the store and raw on the real-time plane: the run queue per vCPU, the disk queue divided by 100. A join across planes scales per key first live.

What it settles for the design

  • A warehouse that extracts the store's mean and peak keys and the hourly MAX rollup keeps what a 20-second feed would give it for these families, without the volume.
  • The real-time plane earns its lane for what the store has no key for: the 2-second tail, per-core hot spots, the contention series of a scoped set within hours.
  • The row key is the same on both planes: sourceId and the object label are the store's (VMEntityVCID, VMEntityObjectID) pair, so the hot-set joins the tiers with no lookup.

What not to do, one line each. No rate() on a gauge: the engine reads a dip as a counter reset. No offset: it parses and does nothing. No subquery in a range call. No unpinned name that serves two cadences. No join across planes without the per-key scale check. No key copied from a picker without the metadata endpoint's word that the source serves it.

The thread through every row

Each tempting option buys the feeling of completeness with load, cost, or a second truth. Each evidence-backed option keeps one source of record per signal and moves the smallest artifact that still carries the decision.

The strategy in one breath

Collect once, at the plane that already owns the signal. Decide in region, where the native data lives. Extract by tier: the gold layer daily, the analysis windows hourly and exact, the hot-set native and scoped. Key everything on the pair every system already carries. Beyond vSphere, one collector per domain, raw in the region, one thin tier each, joined through the CMDB. Bind the catalog to a live statkey list, never to a picker. Treat the real-time plane as the truth for a burst and a troubleshooting store for everything else, read it with its seven rules in code, and remember that its samples are the ones the store already rolled up. Reconcile absence before it becomes a number.
