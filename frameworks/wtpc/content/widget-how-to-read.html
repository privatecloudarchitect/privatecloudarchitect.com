How to read this scorecard
One posture, judged against its own envelope. A green floor with zeros in every count is the state this page exists to hold. When you see that, the weekly review is the only work left. Every other reading is either a gate or a score, and the first skill is telling them apart.
Posture prod-latency-critical-db
SLA Latency-critical (~99.99). A contention event is an outage, not a slowdown.
Tier Production. A floor, performance, or capacity breach pages. A cost breach opens a ticket.
Archetype Data, memory-primary: the RAM belongs to the database. Memory tiering is deliberately withheld. Pinned memory cannot tier, and tiering never combines with overcommit.
Members Everything tagged env=prod · function=db · sla=sla-1: the VMs. Their hosts and clusters follow, derived from where those VMs run.
Gate vs score: the one distinction this page runs on
A gate is a pass or fail check. The availability floor and Cost Coverage % in the cost view are gates. They never award points. When a gate is red, the numbers below it can’t be trusted yet. That doesn’t mean those numbers are bad. It means fix the gate first, then read them.

A score measures distance. Every capacity, performance, and cost signal is compared to this posture’s envelope: target, warn, breach. The farther a signal sits from its target, the worse its color. Gates protect the scores. Scores never affect the gates.

The pillar state is the worst member’s. At this SLA a mean hides the breach the SLA feels. One swapping replica is an outage, no matter how healthy the other forty-nine look. Cluster rows lead with worst-host and worst-VM columns. Every view sorts worst-first, so the top row is the pillar.
Two clocks. The window clock is the banded 95th-percentile columns over the 7-day window. It answers whether the SLA was protected this week. The now clock is the count columns (VMs Swapping, hosts and VMs over breach) and the alerts behind them. It answers who is out right now. One envelope drives both. The percentile judges the week. The counts judge the moment.
How to act
  1. Clear the floor. Any red in the strip outranks everything else. Restore it first.
  2. Work the worst VM. The contention view sorts the p95 offenders to the top. Any swapped GB above zero is an incident now, not a trend.
  3. Work the worst host. In the capacity views, a host past 1.1× DRAM is eroding and a host past 1.25× is out of envelope. Rebalance or de-densify before the pressure reaches the VMs.
  4. Then the money. Read the cost view weekly. Judge Reclaimable % against 5 / 15 / 25. Rank by the GB column when the dollar is blank. Trust the dollars only above the coverage gate.
  5. Finish in the drill-downs. Select a VM to redraw the detail charts. Take the reclaim actions the oversized-candidates view has already ranked. Skip any shrink the VM’s red peak vetoes.
