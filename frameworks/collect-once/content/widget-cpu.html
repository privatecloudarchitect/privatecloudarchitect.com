CPU: demand is the signal, ready and co-stop are the contention
Size from demand; read Ready, Co-stop, and Swap wait as hypervisor scheduling contention no guest agent can see.
Demand (MHz)"Total CPU resources required by the workloads on the virtual machine." The sizing signal; the p95 column is its 95th percentile over the selected 7 days.
Ready (%)"Percentage of time in which the VM was waiting in line to use the CPU on the host."
Co-stop (%)The percentage of time the VM is ready to run but cannot, because of co-scheduling constraints across its vCPUs.
Swap wait (%)"Percentage swap waits for CPU."
Peak columnsthe highest 20-second sample inside the 5-minute cycle, kept in its own key; the mean beside it hides it.
Zeros are values and blanks are absence: swap wait and co-stop read 0 on VMs under no co-scheduling pressure, and every blank cell is a powered-off VM.
What the 5-minute mean loses, measured
Across 36 windows on the reference estate the median peak hidden by the mean was 18 to 25 percent for CPU MHz (p90 39 percent, worst 60 percent) and 72 percent for host CPU contention (worst 4.6 times the mean). The peak keys and the hourly MAX rollup are how the analysis tier keeps it.
Source: Broadcom TechDocs, VCF 9.1, Virtual Machine Metrics (metric definitions quoted verbatim); measured on the reference estate, one VCF 9.1 instance, 2026-09-15 and 2026-09-16 (the Collect Once, Decide at Source charter and its lab records).
